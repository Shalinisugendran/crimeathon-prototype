How IOC Gate decides

Outside threat intel can be genuine, mistaken or an attack in its own right. IOC Gate checks it before anyone blocks anything.

Three checks, in order

  1. Sender check. The From, Reply-To and Return-Path addresses and every link are compared with the supplier’s real domain. Look-alikes, forged headers and replies that leave the supplier’s domain fail. New, privacy-hidden or wrongly owned domains count against the sender.
  2. Harm check. Every entry is tested against our own network ranges, our domains, the supplier’s domain and services we must never block. One harmful entry puts the whole list on hold.
  3. Corroboration. An indicator is only blocked when it shows up in our own logs and the contract contact has confirmed the notice out-of-band. Everything else stays on watch.

The four decisions

REJECT
The notice fails the sender check. Nothing is acted on; the indicators are kept as evidence.
QUARANTINE
At least one entry points at us. The whole list is held for review.
ACCEPT_WATCH
Nothing is harmful, but nothing is confirmed. Indicators are watched, not blocked.
ACCEPT_ENFORCE
Some indicators are seen in our logs and confirmed. Only those go on the blocklist.

What it never does

  • It never connects to the internet. There are no live WHOIS lookups, and links are never opened.
  • It never sends what you paste anywhere. Everything runs in this page.
  • It never puts an indicator on the blocklist without two confirmations.

Known limits

  • Unicode look-alikes beyond its built-in character list can slip past the sender check. The harm and corroboration checks still apply.
  • A hacked but genuine supplier mailbox passes the sender check, so the other two checks have to catch it.
  • Registration data you enter may be out of date. Confirm with the contract contact.
  • Watching first means a real, urgent threat waits for confirmation.
